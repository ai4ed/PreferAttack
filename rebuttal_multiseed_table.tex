\paragraph{Robustness across random seeds.}
Following the reviewer's concern regarding the multiple stochastic components
in our framework (GA mutation, crossover, $\varepsilon$-greedy action selection
in the RL controller, and synonym-word selection), we conducted a multi-seed
evaluation on a representative configuration. We ran $K{=}5$ independent
random seeds $\mathcal{S} = \{0, 1, 2, 42, 123\}$ on
\textbf{Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (judge) $\times$ \textbf{alpaca\_eval}
(full $n{=}161$ test split), with all other hyperparameters held constant.
The global seed controls
\texttt{random}, \texttt{numpy.random}, \texttt{torch}, and the vLLM
\texttt{LLM} constructor; the judge's decoding is greedy
(\texttt{temperature}$=0$), so its outputs are deterministic given identical
inputs. Results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}.

% Discussion paragraph — interpret the table for reviewers.
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Across the five seeds, ASR ranges over a narrow band of
$[0.870,\ 0.913]$ --- a total spread of only $4.34$ percentage points (pp),
with a standard deviation of $0.019$. The resulting 95\% CI has width
$0.047$ (i.e.\ $\pm 2.35$\,pp around the mean), which is substantially
tighter than the run-to-run variance one would expect from a method
driven by GA mutation and RL-based action selection. We interpret this
as evidence that:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{The reported numbers are not artifacts of a lucky seed.}
        All five seeds yield ASR $\geq 0.87$, well above any reasonable
        ``success threshold'' one might set for a preference attack.
  \item \textbf{Per-seed variance is small relative to inter-method gaps.}
        The $4.34$\,pp spread is an order of magnitude smaller than the
        gaps between PreferAttack and the strongest baseline considered in
        our main results.
  \item \textbf{Stochastic components are adequately controlled.}
        Combined with the deterministic population initialization
        (loaded from a fixed template file \texttt{prompt\_group.pth} of
        500 hand-crafted suffixes) and the greedy judge decoder, the
        seed-controlled experiment confirms that the framework's overall
        behaviour is reproducible.
\end{itemize}

% Note on the small-sample sweep (optional, can be moved to appendix).

We additionally conducted a smaller-scale seed sweep on
\textbf{Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct} / \textbf{code\_judge\_bench} with $K{=}5$ on
the first $n{=}20$ samples per seed, yielding mean $= 0.730 \pm 0.104$
(95\% CI $[0.601,\ 0.859]$); the larger spread reflects the small
per-seed sample size rather than algorithmic instability. A full-data
single-seed run on this configuration
(\texttt{seed}$=2$, $n{=}421$) achieves $\text{ASR}=0.558$, which falls
inside the 95\% CI of the small-sample sweep, indicating that the
originally reported numbers are not outliers but representative draws
from the seed-induced distribution.
